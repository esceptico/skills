# Design process

Use this when shaping a product or flow, not for a narrow visual correction.

## Work from meaning outward

Make decisions in this dependency order:

1. **Purpose and structure:** Define the primary value, content model, and major areas. Remove features that compete with the purpose.
2. **Navigation:** Make location, destinations, and return paths predictable. Keep global destinations few and distinct; place contextual actions with their content.
3. **Content:** Separate unlike information, group by human relevance, and reveal secondary detail progressively. Use real representative content before judging the layout.
4. **Visual design:** Use hierarchy, type, imagery, semantic color, and spacing to reinforce the earlier decisions and establish an appropriate emotional tone.
5. **Interaction polish:** Add feedback and motion that explain causality, state, and spatial continuity. Polish must not compensate for unclear structure.

For every important view, answer without explanation:

- Where am I?
- What can I do here?
- Where can I go next, and how do I return?

If the interface cannot answer these from visible structure and familiar behavior, repair that before adding instruction.

## Prototype and evaluate

- Prototype the uncertain interaction, not merely the appearance. Include realistic content, interruption, empty, loading, error, success, and recovery states that could change the decision.
- Use the cheapest prototype that exposes the uncertainty, then interact with it. A static mockup cannot validate focus, navigation, direct manipulation, interruption, or motion.
- Test the riskiest assumption early with representative people and contexts. Treat preference as weaker evidence than observed success, confusion, or failure on a realistic task.
- Preserve the product’s strongest coherent patterns. Iterate on demonstrated problems instead of redesigning every surface.

## Optional aspiration lens

For broad product critique, the Apple Design Awards categories can prompt useful questions:

- **Interaction:** Are controls effortless and suited to the platform?
- **Inclusivity:** Can people with varied abilities, backgrounds, and languages participate with autonomy?
- **Innovation:** Does novelty materially improve the experience?
- **Social impact:** Does the product improve lives without exploiting attention or trust?
- **Visuals and graphics:** Is the visual system distinctive, coherent, legible, and well executed?
- **Delight and fun:** Is the experience memorable and satisfying because the whole works well?

Do not turn these categories into a numeric score or require every product to optimize every category.
